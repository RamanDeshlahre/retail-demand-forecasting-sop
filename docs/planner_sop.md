# Planner operating guide

## Daily refresh

1. Check the feed date, item/store uniqueness, availability and price coverage. Escalate missing or duplicated data before publishing a forecast.
2. Run the established as-of forecast and retain model version, source versions and issue timestamp. Keep the previous published forecast for audit.
3. Inspect signed bias, WAPE and the largest unit and approximate value exceptions by item, store and category. Distinguish genuine demand shifts from ranging, availability and data errors.
4. Ask Commercial to confirm planned event, price and promotion inputs. Ask Supply Chain to confirm lead time, case size and supplier limits. Ask Distribution to confirm receiving/transport constraints.
5. Record every adjustment with item, store, period, old value, new value, reason, evidence, owner and approval time. Publish the approved forecast and the feasible supply plan as distinct versions.

## Weekly S&OP

1. Compare forecast demand with supplier and distribution capacity at department and item-store level. Show both base and stress assumptions.
2. Agree constrained allocation priorities, customer availability risk, expiry/waste exposure and the owner of each unresolved exception.
3. Review forecast performance at daily, weekly and 28-day horizons. A model that wins at one horizon may lose at another.
4. Score forecast value added when actuals arrive: compare the untouched statistical forecast, the final override and the simple baseline on the same dates.
5. Update the SOP and train colleagues using one concrete item-store example and a written explanation of uncertainty.

## Escalation examples

- Missing item/store feed or unexplained zero streak: verify stock and ranging data before changing forecast.
- Promotional uplift exceeds confirmed supply: agree a capped allocation and document expected lost availability.
- High positive bias for short-life items: challenge incoming orders and record waste exposure.
- Capacity change over a weekend: inform the rota owner and log the decision time for the next planning forum.
